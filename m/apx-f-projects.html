Project Ideas

Forty projects, graded by difficulty, each with a criterion that says when it is done.

on this page

How to use these

Every project below has a measurable acceptance criterion — a number, a tolerance, and a protocol. That is deliberate: a project without one turns into an unbounded polish loop, and the criterion is what converts "I built a thing" into "I can show you a number". If a criterion seems arbitrary, replace it with one you can defend, and write the replacement down before you start.

The type column matters. Implement projects teach you how something works by making you build it. Measure projects teach you what is true by making you quantify it — these are the ones that produce transferable judgement. Write projects teach you what you actually showed. Aim for at least one of each per difficulty band; a curriculum of only implementations produces people who can rebuild known systems and cannot evaluate new ones.

Difficulty is 1 (an afternoon) to 5 (two to four weeks). The bands correspond roughly to: 1–2 after M01–M08, 3 after M12–M16, 4–5 after M18 plus the systems modules.

If you only do five

If time allows only five projects, take 8 (MLP with manual backprop), 14 (tokenizer), 19 (train a language model), 22 (DPO) and 40 (write it up). That sequence touches the mathematics, the data pipeline, the training loop, the alignment objective, and the communication of a result — which is the shape of the whole field, compressed.

What makes a criterion measurable

A criterion is measurable when four things are fixed: the quantity (accuracy, bits per byte, p95 latency, speedup), the threshold (≥ 65 percent, < 1e-5, ≥ 2.5×), the protocol (which dataset, which concurrency, how many seeds, how many warmup iterations), and the comparison class (against what baseline, tuned how). "Fast inference" fails all four. "TPOT p95 under 60 ms at concurrency 8 on 512-token prompts, measured over 200 requests after 20 warmup requests, against a naive no-cache implementation" fails none.

Two habits make the difference between a project and a portfolio piece. First, write the criterion before the code, in the repository, with a date — a criterion chosen after seeing the result is not a criterion. Second, when you miss it, record the number you got and the reason you stopped: "missed; 1.8× against a 2.5× target because the baseline was already fused" is a better artefact than a silent rewrite of the target, and it is the sentence a reviewer will actually believe.
